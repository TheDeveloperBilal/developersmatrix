## B. Product Hunt launch

Do this **once**, properly, not as a drive by submission. One prepared launch
is worth more than ten directory listings.

**Prepare a week ahead:**
- Gallery: 4 images. The input screen, a filled report, the category
  breakdown, the PDF export.
- A 30 second screen recording of a real audit running start to finish.
- Ship on a Tuesday or Wednesday, 12:01am Pacific.

**Tagline (60 chars max):**
> Grade any website in 30 seconds. No signup, no email gate.

**Description:**
> I got tired of website graders that show you a score and then hold the
> actual report hostage behind an email form.
>
> So I built one that does not. Paste a URL, get the grade and the full
> breakdown on the same screen. Around 150 checks across eight categories,
> every failure paired with a specific fix, exportable as text or PDF.
>
> It runs on the rendered HTML, so it works the same on WordPress, Shopify,
> Next.js or anything else.
>
> Free, and it stays free. I built it for my own client work and there is no
> reason to gate it.

**First comment (post it yourself immediately after launch):**
> Maker here. Two things worth knowing before you try it.
>
> First, most sites score between 60 and 80 on first run. If you land there
> you are normal. What matters is which category is pulling the average down,
> because that tells you where an afternoon of work actually pays.
>
> Second, a high grade does not guarantee rankings. I have seen sites at 95
> get outranked by sites at 70 with better content. Treat the score as a list
> of things that might be holding you back, not a number to max out.
>
> Happy to answer anything about how the checks are weighted.

## C. Journalist requests (highest value per hour)

Sign up free: **Qwoted** and **Featured** (formerly Terkel). Also check
**SourceBottle**.

Set alerts for: SEO, website performance, Core Web Vitals, AI tools,
developer productivity, web development, freelancing.

Answer 3 to 5 per week. Most take 15 minutes. A placement on a real
publication is worth more than your entire current backlink profile.

**Your credible angles, use these and nothing you cannot back up:**
1. Rebuilding a site's technical SEO from scratch, including what did not work
2. Cutting a Next.js JavaScript bundle by 61% and what actually caused the bloat
3. Why most website audit scores mislead people
4. Spotting AI agent traffic in Search Console and why it distorts your numbers
5. What a small site owner should do when impressions collapse

Angle 4 is genuinely underreported and you now have first hand data on it.
That is your strongest pitch.

**Response template:**
> [Answer the question in the first two sentences. No preamble.]
>
> [One specific detail, number or example from your own work.]
>
> [One sentence of practical takeaway.]
>
> Syed Bilal Shah, founder, DevelopersMatrix (https://developersmatrix.com)

Keep answers under 200 words. Editors cut long ones.

## D. Guest posts

Three or four on real blogs beats thirty anywhere else. Pitch the angle, not
the link.

**Targets worth pitching:** Search Engine Journal (contributor programme),
Search Engine Land, Moz community, Ahrefs blog, Semrush blog, Smashing
Magazine, CSS Tricks, LogRocket blog, DEV community (self publish), freeCodeCamp.

**Pitch email:**

> Subject: Pitch: the Search Console metric that lies to small site owners
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I run developersmatrix.com. Over the last three months I watched my
> impressions swing from 1,000 a day to 3 a day and back, twice, with no
> ranking change behind either move.
>
> The cause was AI agents and scrapers issuing machine formatted queries.
> Things like a leading percent sign, or eight chained site exclusions in one
> query. They generate impressions and never click, so CTR reads as zero and
> the owner panics about a penalty that does not exist.
>
> I have the Search Console exports, the actual query strings, and the
> position data showing rankings improved while impressions collapsed.
>
> I would like to write this up for [publication], around 1,200 words, with
> the real screenshots and a short method for telling bot queries from human
> ones.
>
> Happy to send an outline first.
>
> Syed Bilal Shah
> https://developersmatrix.com

That pitch works because you have evidence almost nobody else has bothered to
collect. Lead with it.

## E. Community, no follow but they seed real links

- **r/SEO, r/juststart, r/bigseo**: share the audit tool once, in a thread
  where someone is asking for one. Never as a standalone post.
- **Indie Hackers**: a build log post about the tool.
- **Hacker News Show HN**: one shot, Tuesday morning Pacific.
- **Dev.to and Hashnode**: cross post your best articles with a canonical tag
  pointing back to the original on your domain.

These are nofollow. They matter because they produce the secondary coverage
that does get followed.

## What not to do

- Anything advertised as a link package, link building service or PBN
- Fiverr and similar gig sites, without exception
- Blog comment links
- More profiles on UGC social networks
- Reciprocal link swaps
- Paid directories

You already have 34 domains being disavowed because of this category of link.
Do not add to the pile.
